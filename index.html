Project 2 Persuasive or Deceptive Visualization?

Ella Li, Lydia Chin, Amelia Oo, Bianca Grunbaum — ell024@ucsd.edu, lychin@ucsd.edu, amoo@ucsd.edu, bgrunbaum@ucsd.edu

Proposition: "Gender equality in education has been achieved."

FOR the Proposition

Design Decisions and Rationale:

  • Data transformation: We calculated a gap field by subtracting boys' secondary enrollment from girls' for each year (in percentage points), binned each year into a decade column, and grouped by decade to compute the average gap. Showing decade averages of +0.8, +0.8, and +0.3 points condenses the story into a few small numbers that read as "essentially equal."
  • Marks and encodings: Two lines, separated by color (blue for boys, orange for girls), show enrollment on the y-axis against year on the x-axis. The gap between them is shaded gray so viewers see the difference directly, and that sliver of gray stays thin across the whole chart.
  • Visual design: We started the chart in 1990, when the gap was already small, and chose secondary rather than college enrollment, where the two groups are closest. The y-axis starts at 80% rather than 0, which keeps both lines near the top of the chart and frames the comparison around the high overall enrollment rate.
  • Annotation: Labels call out the largest gap (3.5 points in 1998) and the most recent gap (0.9 points in 2018). Highlighting that even the worst year differs by only a few points reinforces the message that the gap is negligible, and the title states the conclusion outright.

AGAINST the Proposition

Design Decisions and Rationale:

  • Data transformation: We calculated a gap field by subtracting men's college enrollment from women's for each year (in percentage points), binned each year into a decade column, and grouped by decade to compute the average gap. The rising sequence of averages (9.1 → 19.3 → 26.0 → 28.3) supports the title's claim that the gap has grown every decade.
  • Marks and encodings: Lines with point markers show women's and men's enrollment for each year, separated by color (orange for women, blue for men), with position on the y-axis encoding enrollment rate and the x-axis encoding year. A gray shaded area fills the gap, so its widening over time is visible as a growing wedge.
  • Visual design: The chart starts in 1979, the year women passed men, leaving out earlier years when men were ahead so the gap only ever appears to widen. The y-axis starts at 50 rather than 0, which stretches the vertical distance between the lines and makes the gap look larger.
  • Annotation: Each decade is labeled with its average gap, and dotted lines mark the decade boundaries so the stepwise growth is easy to follow. A caption explains that "pts" means percentage points (women's rate minus men's rate).
